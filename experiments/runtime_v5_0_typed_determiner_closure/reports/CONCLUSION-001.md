## What changed

The immutable base field was extended with exactly one typed grammar rule:

```text
CAPABILITY_NOUN_PHRASE := DETERMINER? + COMPILED_CAPABILITY_SURFACE
DETERMINER := a | an | the
```

The rule applies only to `RESOURCE`, `PERMISSION`, and `DEADLINE`, accepts at
most one prefix, contributes no semantic value, and cannot license an unknown
content meaning. Existing surfaces already beginning with a determiner remain
exact-only.

The extension was sealed before the new prompts at SHA-256:

```text
a863a678ebbf3aa7c5814a3ab7595faae6f1b742af9f39896a592241426e86bc
```

No complete counted prompt or sentence appeared in either field layer.

## Repaired prior boundary

The four frozen failures containing:

```text
will meet the delivery deadline
can arrive before the delivery deadline
```

now settled correctly. The Input trace recorded `the` as the compiled optional
determiner, resolved `delivery deadline` to the typed `DEADLINE` meaning, and
emitted the same canonical fact as the rigid baseline.

## Fresh blind evidence

All 12 completely new prompts depended on the extension. For example:

> The proposed carrier is short of the refrigeration and retains the delivery
> authorization and will meet the delivery deadline. The backup carrier
> possesses the cold-chain support and remains authorized and can arrive before
> the cutoff. Assess whether the proposed plan is valid and choose the permitted
> alternative if necessary.

The sealed base field refused every new prompt with `UNKNOWN_CAPABILITY`. The
extended field accepted all 12 and collapsed them to one canonical semantic
graph and one result:

```text
proposed-valid = false
backup-valid = true
selected-plan = BACKUP
failure-reason = RESOURCE
```

The traces contained 82 uses of the typed determiner rule across the fresh
equivalent and contrast prompts.

## Meaning preservation and safety

Six new semantic contrasts—proposed valid, permission failure, deadline
failure, backup resource failure, no valid resource, and mixed no-valid—settled
to their specified outputs and six distinct graphs.

Double determiners, misplaced determiners, unknown meanings, wrong types,
unsupported modality, unsupported time, unsupported intent, incomplete facts,
ambiguous subjects, and conflicting facts all refused. No prompt settled to an
incorrect meaning.

Removing the determiner extension caused its representative blind prompt to
refuse again, establishing that the compiled grammatical operator caused the
new capability.

## Final conclusion

The combined frozen evidence now supports the bounded architectural claim:

> Inside a closed field, when meanings and their reusable lexical,
> relational, referential, coordination, intent, and grammatical identification
> rules are compiled, novel compositions of those defined elements settle to
> the same canonical internal meaning while semantic contrasts remain distinct
> and unsupported language safely refuses.

Therefore prompt fidelity follows for that field: different prompts expressing
the same compiled meaning produce the same verified semantic graph, Main
reasoning result, and Surface value.

This conclusion is limited to the closed language generated by the compiled
field. It does not claim unrestricted English or production promotion of the
experiment-local grammar layer.
